How I think

Marketing isn't about being seen.
It's about being chosen.

Big following. Pretty posts. Lots of activity. But the phone isn't ringing. If that's your business right now, the problem isn't your design or your captions. It's the logic underneath everything.

Most of the marketing playbooks that exist were written for markets where institutional trust is high — where people trust banks, brands, and systems by default. In Lesotho, trust works differently. It's built through people, through community, through consistency over time. Businesses that try to copy Western marketing tactics without adapting for this reality burn budget and wonder what went wrong. I build for how our people actually make decisions.

01
⚙
Systems over content

Content without a system behind it is just entertainment. The system is what converts attention into customers — and it has to be built deliberately.

A content system maps the journey from "stranger who just saw your ad" to "paid customer." It includes trust-building touchpoints, a clear offer structure, a reason to act now, and a follow-up mechanism. Without this, even viral content produces nothing lasting.

02
🌍
Africa-first, not Africa-last

The best marketing strategy for a business in Maseru is not the same one that works in Manchester. The psychology is different. The trust architecture is different.

This means building content that acknowledges local values, using Sesotho where it deepens connection, understanding that WhatsApp is infrastructure not a backup channel, and recognising that community validation drives decisions more than brand polish here.

03
📈
Revenue, not vanity

Likes, followers, reach — these are inputs, not outcomes. The only number that matters at the end of the month is revenue. I design toward that number.

Every piece of strategy I build is measured against one question: does this move someone closer to paying? That doesn't mean every post is a sales pitch — it means every piece of content has a role in the larger system that ends in a sale.
